Kerala Robusta: what the production numbers mean for your café menu

Ploomi Journal · Research checked 15 November 2026

Kerala is more than a market for coffee. It is a major coffee-growing state, and Robusta makes up the great majority of its estimated production. For a café buyer, the useful next question is how an individual coffee performs in the drinks you actually serve.

Start with what the data says

The Coffee Board of India’s final estimate for crop year 2025–26 puts Kerala’s production at 80,350 metric tonnes: 78,275 tonnes of Robusta and 2,075 tonnes of Arabica. The Board lists Wayanad at 68,800 tonnes of Robusta. These are production estimates, not a count of specialty-grade coffee or proof that a particular roaster uses Kerala coffee. See the Coffee Board table.

The same table presents a post-blossom estimate for 2026–27. That is a forecast and should not be read as a completed harvest. Kerala’s Wayanad Robusta also has a geographical indication; that identifies its geographical connection, but it does not establish that every lot tastes the same. Read the Government of India note.

What does this mean for the drink?

Species and region are useful starting points, but variety, growing conditions, processing, roast, freshness and brewing all matter. Avoid choosing a coffee solely because a bag says “Robusta” or “Arabica”. Ask for the coffee’s origin, processing, roast date and recommended brewing approach, then judge the specific sample.

If your menu is mostly milk drinks, taste the coffee both black and in the milk drink customers order. Note whether its flavour remains clear at your usual milk-to-coffee ratio, whether the finish suits the menu, and how repeatable the result is. A different menu may call for a different choice: the coffee that works in a cappuccino need not be the one you want for filter coffee.

A useful comparison for your team

  1. Ask suppliers for details and samples of the actual coffees available, including whether a blend contains Robusta.
  2. Prepare each sample using a consistent starting recipe and record dose and yield. Adjust where needed; different coffees may need different settings.
  3. Compare aroma, flavour, texture and aftertaste before deciding which you prefer. The SCA’s Coffee Value Assessment separates description from preference for this reason.
  4. Make the most popular milk drink and taste again as it cools. Record the recipe and ask two or more team members to share observations.
  5. Discuss supply, order frequency, quotation and delivery separately from taste.

A local origin can be a meaningful story when the supplier can verify the details. It is still worth asking about the specific lot and tasting it on the bar.
